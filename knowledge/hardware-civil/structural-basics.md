# Civil and structural design basics (India-focused)

## Indian Standards commonly used
- IS 456:2000 — Plain and reinforced concrete.
- IS 800:2007 — General construction in steel.
- IS 875 (Parts 1–5) — Design loads: dead (Part 1), imposed (Part 2), wind (Part 3), snow (Part 4), and special loads and combinations (Part 5).
- IS 1893 (Part 1):2016 — Earthquake-resistant design; IS 13920:2016 — ductile detailing of RC structures.
- IS 10262:2019 — Concrete mix proportioning; IS 1786 — high-strength deformed steel bars (Fe415, Fe500, Fe500D, etc.).

## Materials
- Concrete grades are named by characteristic compressive strength: M20 = 20 MPa, M25 = 25 MPa. IS 456 gives the short-term modulus Ec = 5000·√fck MPa (about 25 GPa for M25). M20 is the minimum grade for reinforced concrete in mild exposure.
- Unit weights (typical): reinforced concrete 25 kN/m³, plain concrete 24 kN/m³, brick masonry about 19–20 kN/m³.
- Imposed load on residential floors is typically 2 kN/m² (IS 875 Part 2).

## Limit state design
Factored load for dead + imposed loads is 1.5 × (DL + LL). Partial safety factors for materials: 1.5 for concrete and 1.15 for steel. Serviceability: final deflection is commonly limited to span/250, and span/350 or 20 mm (whichever is less) after partitions and finishes.

## Simply supported beam with uniformly distributed load w over span L
Reactions = wL/2; maximum moment = wL²/8 at mid-span; maximum shear = wL/2 at supports; mid-span deflection = 5wL⁴/(384EI).

## Important
Foundations require a geotechnical investigation (soil bearing capacity). Real structures must be designed, checked and approved by a qualified structural engineer under local building regulations.
