# Biomarkers in medical research

## Definition and categories
A biomarker is a defined characteristic measured as an indicator of normal biological processes, pathogenic processes or responses to an exposure or intervention. The FDA–NIH BEST (Biomarkers, EndpointS, and other Tools) resource defines seven categories: susceptibility/risk, diagnostic, monitoring, prognostic, predictive, pharmacodynamic/response, and safety biomarkers.

## Types by source
Molecular (proteins, metabolites, nucleic acids, genetic variants), imaging, physiological (e.g. heart rate variability from wearables, called digital biomarkers when collected by digital devices) and histological.

## Validation pathway
1. Analytical validation: does the assay measure the biomarker accurately and reproducibly (precision, accuracy, limits of detection, stability)?
2. Clinical validation: does the biomarker reliably relate to the clinical state or outcome in the intended population (sensitivity, specificity, AUC, calibration)?
3. Clinical utility: does using it improve decisions or outcomes compared with current practice?
Discovery studies often overfit; independent external validation cohorts are essential.

## Example: diabetes-related biomarkers studied in research
Glycated haemoglobin (HbA1c), fasting plasma glucose, oral glucose tolerance test values, C-peptide (insulin secretion), islet autoantibodies such as GAD65 (autoimmune diabetes), fructosamine and glycated albumin (shorter-term glycaemia), urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio (kidney involvement), and emerging omics signatures (metabolomics, proteomics). Continuous glucose monitoring metrics such as time in range are increasingly used as research outcomes.
