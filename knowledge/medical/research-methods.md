# Medical research methods and evidence appraisal

## Hierarchy of evidence (typical)
1. Systematic reviews and meta-analyses of randomised controlled trials (RCTs)
2. Individual RCTs
3. Cohort studies (prospective, then retrospective)
4. Case-control studies
5. Cross-sectional studies and case series
6. Expert opinion, mechanistic and laboratory studies
Quality within a level matters as much as the level: a poorly conducted RCT can be less reliable than a well-designed cohort study. The GRADE approach rates certainty of evidence as high, moderate, low or very low.

## Framing a research question: PICO
Population, Intervention (or exposure), Comparison, Outcome — sometimes extended with Time and Study design (PICOTS). Example: In adults with type 2 diabetes (P), does continuous glucose monitoring (I) compared with self-monitoring of blood glucose (C) improve HbA1c (O)?

## Bias and confounding
Common biases: selection bias, information/measurement bias, recall bias, attrition bias, publication bias and spin. Confounding occurs when a third variable is associated with both exposure and outcome; randomisation, matching, stratification and multivariable adjustment address it.

## Reporting guidelines
PRISMA (systematic reviews), CONSORT (RCTs), STROBE (observational studies), STARD (diagnostic accuracy), TRIPOD (prediction models), SPIRIT (trial protocols). They are also useful checklists when appraising papers.

## Key statistics
- Sensitivity = TP / (TP + FN); specificity = TN / (TN + FP).
- Positive predictive value = TP / (TP + FP); negative predictive value = TN / (TN + FN). Predictive values depend on prevalence.
- ROC curve and area under the curve (AUC) summarise discrimination across thresholds.
- Relative risk, odds ratio and hazard ratio compare groups; always read them with 95% confidence intervals.
- A p-value is not the probability that a hypothesis is true; clinical significance differs from statistical significance.

## Research ethics
Human studies need ethics committee approval and informed consent. In India, biomedical research follows the ICMR National Ethical Guidelines for Biomedical and Health Research Involving Human Participants, and clinical trials are regulated under the New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules, 2019.
